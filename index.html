About me

I know the gap between graduating
and actually getting hired.

I came to Sydney from Ulaanbaatar to study a Master of Banking and Finance at Macquarie University, after completing my BBA at the National University of Mongolia.

I grew up in a rural Mongolian town with very few foreigners, where opportunities to learn English, access strong education resources, or understand pathways to studying abroad were extremely limited. I remember deciding to learn English from a couple of books on my own because there simply weren't many other options available to us at the time. Collectively, we didn't have the networks, guidance, or exposure to properly understand how to build careers internationally. That shaped my early understanding of opportunity — and how uneven access to information and connections can be.

When I arrived in Australia, I didn't have a map to follow. Like most international students, I had to learn everything through trial and error: how Australian recruiters read a resume, how to talk about my visa without feeling like it was a disadvantage, and how to turn internships and volunteer roles into real job opportunities.

I quickly realised something important: having a Master's degree in Australia does not automatically place you on the same pathway as local graduates. Recruiters often interpret international experience differently, and you need to be intentional about how you position yourself in a system that wasn't designed with your starting point in mind.

Being a second-language English speaker added another layer of challenge — but it also helped me understand something valuable: you don't need perfect language or a perfect background to create value. What matters is clarity in what you bring, and confidence in how you communicate it. I also understand how difficult it can be to build a professional identity in a place where you don't yet feel fully confident or naturally "belong," especially when you're surrounded by people who haven't lived through the same struggle.

Since graduating, I've worked across financial services and infrastructure advisory in Sydney and Perth — as a Credit Analyst and Business Development Manager at SCG Finance, a Financial Viability Analyst at Equifax Australia, and currently as a Consultant at Paxon Group, working on healthcare infrastructure and project finance across Asia Pacific and the Middle East.

While studying my Master's, I started unofficially mentoring and tutoring fellow students, which naturally grew into helping dozens of people with CV reviews, interview preparation, and job search strategy alongside my studies and full-time work. I also tried building things from scratch — from starting a student association at Macquarie University to exploring entrepreneurship in Australia. In many ways, I was trying to break ceilings on my own, often spending unnecessary time and energy figuring things out the hard way.

Looking back, what I wish I had most was someone who had already walked the same path — someone who could shorten the learning curve, explain how things actually work, and help me avoid the trial-and-error phase. That is why I do this now. Because I've seen how even a small shift in mindset, or a clearer way of presenting yourself, can completely change the direction of someone's career. I want to be that person for others who are going through the same journey alone — and help them move forward with clarity, confidence, and direction.
